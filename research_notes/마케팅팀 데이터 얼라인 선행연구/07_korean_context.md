## Q1. What do Korean studies and reports say about Korean organizational culture barriers (hierarchy, 보고 문화, voice and silence, 야근 and overwork)?

### Takeaway
Three waves of large 대한상공회의소 surveys (2016, 2018 and 2020; the first two with McKinsey) give a consistent diagnosis.
- Korean work methods score at "failing" levels on the dimensions that matter most for a shared data practice:
  - **업무 방향성 30/100**, **지시 명확성 39/100**, **추진 자율성 37/100** (2018).
  - Reporting and meetings were rated 41–55/100 across 2016–2018.
- The single most-cited reason work feels irrational is that it was **"meaningless from the start" (원래부터 의미없는 업무, 50.9%)**.
- The root causes the reports name are "leadership without deliberation or consultation" and "communication without explanation or questions" (설명·질문 없는 소통문화).
- Younger and lower-ranked employees are the most critical. Only **21.3% of employees in their 30s** (vs 51.9% in their 50s) say leaders' instructions are clear.

This maps closely onto the user's symptom: "Do we really have to do this too?" Korean silence research adds that withholding input is often a **strategic, defensive or resigned choice** shaped by how supervisors react, not mere passivity.

### Cited Findings

**대한상의 × McKinsey, first diagnosis (2016): 「한국기업의 조직건강도와 기업문화 종합보고서」 — Grade B (large survey, press-reported)**
- Sample and method: 100 Korean companies, about 40,000 employees, 9 months of fieldwork. [뉴스토마토 (2016)](https://newstomato.com/ReadNews.aspx?no=634567); [시대 (2016)](https://www.sidae.com/article/2016062320188087741)
- **77 of 100 companies** scored below global benchmarks on McKinsey's Organizational Health Index. [뉴스토마토 (2016)](https://newstomato.com/ReadNews.aspx?no=634567)
- Employee favorability scores (0–100) for culture elements:
  - **습관적 야근 31** (the lowest)
  - **비효율적 회의 39**
  - **과도한 보고 41**
  - The report called meetings and reporting "drivers of overtime". It also noted many complaints about one-way instructions without dialogue (소통 없는 일방적 업무지시) and 상명하복 불통문화.
  - Sources: [뉴스토마토 (2016)](https://newstomato.com/ReadNews.aspx?no=634567); [inews24](https://www.inews24.com/view/947237)
- **"야근의 역설" — Grade C, because it is a small observational sub-study.** Researchers observed the workdays of 45 employees at 8 companies. Habitual overtime workers worked longer hours but had **45% productivity vs 57%** for the others. Do not generalize from this. [search summary of 2016 coverage: 뉴스토마토](https://newstomato.com/ReadNews.aspx?no=634567)

**대한상의 × McKinsey, second diagnosis (May 2018) — Grade B (press-reported)**
- Sample: about 2,000 large-company employees, plus an Organizational Health Index analysis of 8 companies (3 large, 3 mid-sized, 2 startups).
- Change in scores, 2016 → 2018:

| Element | 2016 | 2018 |
|---|---|---|
| 야근 | 31 | 46 |
| 회의 | 39 | 47 |
| 보고 | 41 | 55 |
| 업무지시 | 55 | 65 |
| 회식 (the only "excellent" item) | 77 | 85 |

- **87.8%** of respondents judged the improvement negatively. Only **12.2%** said things had fundamentally improved.
- **7 of 8** companies were weaker than global peers, and none reached the top tier.
- Press framed the results as "청바지 입은 꼰대" (a boss in jeans, i.e. casual-dress reform with unchanged hierarchy) and "무늬만 혁신" (innovation in name only).
- Figures are consistent across outlets: [아주경제](https://www.ajunews.com/view/20180514161639260); [뉴시스](https://mobile.newsis.com/view/NISX20180514_0000307416); [뉴스토마토](https://newstomato.com/ReadNews.aspx?no=823903); [디지털타임스](https://www.dt.co.kr/article/11166745)

**대한상의 「국내기업의 업무방식 실태 보고서」 (10 Oct 2018) — Grade B (press-reported)**
- Sample: about 4,000 employees of listed companies, plus interviews with employees and experts.
- Overall score for work methods: **45/100**.
- Sub-scores (all below 50):
  - **업무 방향성 30**
  - **지시 명확성 39** (defined as "the boss explains the background and content of instructions clearly")
  - **추진 자율성 37**
  - **과정 효율성 45**
- Reasons work feels irrational:
  - **원래부터 의미없는 업무 50.9%**
  - "일단 하고 보자" execution habit **47.5%**
  - 상사의 비계획적 업무지시 **38.8%**
- Root causes named by the report: 고민·협의 없는 리더십, and 설명·질문 없는 소통문화.
- **The lower the rank, the more negatively respondents rated the clarity of instructions and feedback.**
- Sources: [뉴스토마토](https://newstomato.com/ReadNews.aspx?no=850599); [이투데이](https://www.etoday.co.kr/news/view/1671350); [전자신문](https://www.etnews.com/20181010000270)

**대한상의 「한국기업의 세대갈등과 기업문화 종합진단 보고서」 (8 Apr 2020) — Grade B (press-reported)**
- Sample: about 13,000 employees at 30 large and mid-sized firms (some outlets say about 12,900), plus generational in-depth interviews.
- **63.9%** of employees feel a generation gap.
- "Leaders' instructions are clear": **51.9%** of employees in their 50s agree vs **21.3%** of those in their 30s.
- Generational labels used in the report:
  - The older generation is the **"지도 세대" (map generation)**: they learned work loosely.
  - The younger generation is the **"내비게이션 세대" (navigation generation)**: they want explicit routes.
- Another contrast in the report: older workers are duty-centred and put assigned work first, while younger workers are rights-centred and emphasize contracted hours.
- Overtime drivers it identified:
  - sudden instructions just before leaving time
  - unclear division of roles
  - **lack of communication about the background or purpose of a task (배경이나 취지에 대한 소통 부족)**
- Prescription: move "from a family-like company to a pro-team company".
- 대한상의 says vertical, top-down work practices push the burden of generational friction onto junior ranks.
- Sources: [이투데이](https://www.etoday.co.kr/news/view/1881067); [경북매일](https://kbmaeil.com/article/202004080275798)

**Title and hierarchy flattening (호칭 파괴) has limited effect — Grade C/D (case reports and expert opinion)**
- Hanwha E&C introduced title-flattening in 2012 and reverted around 2015, citing the need for rank-based tension on safety-critical sites. [아주경제 (2020)](https://www.ajunews.com/view/20201016171315467)
- KAIST professor 이병태 calls title reform low-level but symbolic innovation. Real flattening also requires participation in decisions and changes to evaluation and compensation. [한경비즈니스 (2022)](https://magazine.hankyung.com/business/article/202203094207b)
- Samsung Electronics simplified its ranks to CL1–4 in 2017. [한경비즈니스 (2017)](https://magazine.hankyung.com/business/article/201702214600b)
- CJ introduced the "님" honorific in 2000. [메트로신문](https://www.metroseoul.co.kr/article/20230313500523)

**National culture baseline (Hofstede) — Grade C (classic framework; secondary citations only)**
- South Korea scores **Power Distance 60** (moderate-to-high hierarchy) and **Individualism 18** (among the most collectivist in Hofstede's data). Source: secondary sites that cite Hofstede, e.g. [Cyborlink](https://www.cyborlink.com/besite/korea_south.htm) and a [Salem State teaching handout](https://salemstate.dspace7.openrepository.com/bitstreams/103f2d32-1ac4-4aee-ab14-33807ce92d17/download).
- I could not reach the primary Hofstede Insights page to verify these. Other dimension scores (e.g. Uncertainty Avoidance) are **unverified** and should not be quoted.

**Employee silence and voice in Korean organizations — Grade C (individual Korean survey studies; full text not read)**
- Korean industrial and organizational psychology papers (한국심리학회지: 산업 및 조직) treat silence as a deliberate, strategic choice rather than simply not speaking. They use three types:
  - **체념적 (acquiescent)**
  - **방어적 (defensive)**
  - **친사회적 (prosocial)**
- Acquiescent silence is described as arising from how management and supervisors treat silence and from how few chances to communicate there are.
- One study used confirmatory factor analysis on 204 employees who work closely with a supervisor. It tested whether silence is distinct from the mere absence of voice.
- Sources: [KSIOP journal 27(2):291](https://accesson.kr/ksiop/assets/pdf/19833/journal-27-2-291.pdf); [KoreaScience JAKO201718857601232](https://koreascience.or.kr/article/JAKO201718857601232.pdf). The search summary did not cleanly attribute each claim to one of the two papers. Treat them as a pair.
- **A Korean meta-analysis of organizational silence exists, but only among nurses** (Grade A in its domain, low transfer to marketing). [J Korean Acad Soc Nurs Educ](https://jkasne.org/journal/view.php?number=998). I did not retrieve its effect sizes.

**Psychological safety in Korea — Grade C**
- A survey of 454 R&D employees at Korean firms, analysed with structural equation modelling, linked psychological safety with work grit, knowledge-sharing intention and innovative behavior. The study did not test leader inclusiveness. [국회도서관 KINX2026044734](https://dl.nanet.go.kr/detail/KINX2026044734)
- **Non-Korean cross-reference, Grade B:** a randomized trial in 500+ teams at a global healthcare firm found that manager conversations focused on employees' psychological needs raised team psychological safety. [CEPR DP20218](https://cepr.org/publications/dp20218)

### Inferences
- The Korean barrier is **not mainly unwillingness to use data**. It is a *work-method* problem: unclear direction (30/100), unclear instructions (39/100), low autonomy (37/100), and tasks that feel "meaningless from the start" (50.9%). A new data-collection requirement handed down top-down **without its background and purpose** fits the exact pattern 대한상의 identifies as the main source of perceived irrationality and overtime. The team's "이것까지 해야 하나요?" is a predictable response, not a personality problem.
- The generation gap in perceived instruction clarity (30s: 21.3% vs 50s: 51.9%) suggests a team lead who feels they explained the purpose may be systematically overestimating how clear it was. The "navigation generation" wants explicit definitions: what to collect, why, in what format, and what decision it will feed.
- High reporting burden (보고 41→55) and meeting inefficiency mean data work is likely to be *experienced as one more report*. Design should put data work **in place of** some existing reporting or meeting time, not on top of it. Otherwise it reads as 업무과중.
- Silence research implies that weak or misdirected analysis may partly reflect **defensive or resigned silence**. Team members may not ask "why this metric?" or say "this data is not useful for my channel". The lead needs structured, low-risk ways to get disagreement into the open, such as anonymous pre-reads or a "devil's advocate" role. Assuming that silence means agreement is risky.
- Symbolic flattening (titles, casual dress) did not move the scores much ("청바지 입은 꼰대"). What matters is real participation in deciding *which* questions the data should answer. This supports co-defining goals and metrics with the team rather than handing over a predefined template.
- **For the user's team:** start by explaining the *why* (the decision each metric will inform). Co-define the metric set with channel owners (performance, content/SNS, brand/CRM). Replace an existing report instead of adding one. Explicitly invite "this is useless" feedback.

### Gaps
- I found no 대한상의 or McKinsey organizational-health follow-up after 2020, and no 2022–2026 update of the 업무방식 scores. Recent shifts after remote work and AI adoption are therefore not covered.
- I could not open the original 대한상의 PDFs. All numbers are press-reported, and sample sizes differ slightly across outlets (about 12,900 vs about 13,000 in 2020).
- No Korean study isolated *marketing* teams, or small teams of 3–8 people, for hierarchy, voice or silence effects.
- I found no Korean meta-analysis (outside nursing) giving effect sizes for leader behaviour → employee voice or silence. A KCI search for "권력거리 침묵 메타분석" is needed.
- I could not verify Hofstede scores against the primary source.

## Q2. What Korean research exists on 데이터 리터러시, 데이터 기반 의사결정, and data use in marketing organizations?

### Takeaway
Korean academic evidence is thin and mostly cross-sectional.
- The most relevant paper is a 2021 KoreaScience-indexed study (Informatization Policy).
  - Employees' data literacy is positively related to their organization's data-analytics maturity.
  - **Hierarchy culture *buffers* (weakens) that link, while relationship culture amplifies it.**
  - This is direct evidence that literacy training in a hierarchical climate converts poorly into practice.
- National statistics (K-DATA 데이터산업 현황조사) cover the *supply side*, i.e. data-industry firms. They show persistent talent shortages: overall shortage rate 13.7% in 2025, and 33.0% for data scientists.
- A 2026 vendor survey reports that about 60% of Korean firms self-rate as "data-driven". Even so, 61% of the leading firms struggle to connect data to real business priorities, and "company-wide data literacy" is a top challenge.
- Marketing-specific Korean evidence is practitioner-grade only. One survey suggests Korean marketers value **problem definition (67.65%)** far above **data-analysis skill (9.80%)**.

### Cited Findings

**Data literacy × organizational culture → analytics maturity (Korea, 2021) — Grade B/C (cross-sectional survey; N not retrieved)**
- Paper: "Data Literacy, Organizational Culture, and Data Analytics Maturity: Moderating Effect of Organizational Culture", *Informatization Policy*, received Nov 2020, accepted Jan 2021.
- Employees with higher data-literacy competency report higher organizational data-analytics maturity.
- Relationship (clan) culture and innovation (adhocracy) culture are positively related to analytics maturity.
- Moderation: **relationship culture has a synergistic effect, and hierarchy culture has a buffer effect,** on the data literacy → maturity relationship.
- Sources: [KoreaScience JAKO202112362357574 (page)](https://koreascience.kr/article/JAKO202112362357574.page); [PDF](https://koreascience.or.kr/article/JAKO202112362357574.pdf)

### Inferences
- The 2021 Korean finding that **hierarchy culture buffers the literacy → maturity link** is the most directly relevant academic result. Sending the team to SQL or GA4 training, or giving them templates, will convert poorly into better analysis if the surrounding climate stays "boss defines, team executes". Relationship and innovation cultures amplify literacy. That argues for collaborative sense-making rituals: joint interpretation sessions, and hypotheses proposed by team members.
- Korean data-talent shortage figures (13.7% overall; about 31–33% for data scientists) mean a small marketing team realistically will **not get a dedicated analyst**. The practice has to be lightweight and owned by marketers: a few shared metrics, a common template, spreadsheet-level tools. The finding that Excel/Sheets reports are the dominant task (D-grade) is consistent with this.
- The "self-rated data-driven 60% vs 61% can't connect data to business priorities" gap (D-grade) mirrors the user's problem. Teams *collect* data, but the link from data to decision is missing. The lever is defining the decision before the data.
- Korean marketers rank problem definition (67.65%) far above analysis skill (9.80%) (D-grade, unverified sample). So framing data work as a **problem-definition tool** may fit their professional identity better than a "data-literacy" frame. That is the user's design-thinking territory.
- **For the user's team:** run co-creation sessions to agree the 1–3 decisions each channel needs to make. Map metrics to those decisions. Teach analysis *inside* the team's real questions, not as abstract literacy. Keep tooling at spreadsheet level, or use an AI natural-language query layer.

### Gaps
- I did not find KCI, DBpia or RISS papers specifically on *marketing team* data-driven decision-making or data literacy in Korea. Direct database searches on 마케팅 데이터 활용, 데이터 리터러시 마케터 and 데이터 기반 의사결정 조직문화 are needed.
- I found no validated Korean workplace data-literacy scale.
- I found no 정보통신정책연구원 (KISDI) study on enterprise or marketing data use. The searches returned nothing.
- K-DATA surveys measure data-*industry* firms. I found no national statistic on how *non-data* firms' marketing departments use data.
- The sample size and effect sizes of the 2021 Informatization Policy paper were not retrieved.
- The Salesforce and i-boss figures are vendor or practitioner surveys with unclear sampling.

## Q3. Which Korean company practices are publicly documented (Toss, 우아한형제들/배민, 당근, 쿠팡), and what lessons apply to a small marketing team?

### Takeaway
Public Korean evidence is company self-description: interviews, job postings, conference talks, and articles by employees. It is **Grade D** and subject to employer-branding bias. Still, a consistent pattern appears across companies:
- **Toss** frames data, not top-down authority, as the basis for decisions. It turns *team goals into metrics, hypotheses and key levers*. It organizes small autonomous squads ("silos" of 6–8) with analysts embedded. It invests in shared, trusted metric definitions.
- **당근** practitioners describe moving from "ship ideas from team chats" to experiment-based decisions. A PM routine of daily dashboard checks feeds hypotheses that are shared with the team. Practitioners candidly admit that even data-savvy PMs skip experiments, and that experimentation tends to stay "the work of a few" in Korea.
- **배민** reports that most staff said data matters but felt **fatigue handling data**. An internal AI agent ("물어보세") lowered that friction: active users rose by more than 50% and monthly uses passed 20,000.
- I found nothing public on 쿠팡's data culture.

### Cited Findings

**Toss (비바리퍼블리카 and affiliates) — Grade D (company content and job postings)**
- **Official Toss Feed interview with the Data Analysis chapter:**
  - Toss says it uses data, not top-down decisions by superiors, as its decision criterion.
  - It describes analysts as the bridge between business and tech roles that "speak different languages".
  - Source: [Toss Feed](https://toss.im/tossfeed/article/data-analyst-interview)
- **DataOps Manager posting:** products are built by "silos" of **6–8 people** (PO, designer, developers, data analyst) working autonomously. The data organization owns the full data lifecycle so the company can decide on trustworthy data. [inthiswork](https://inthiswork.com/?p=400009)
- **Growth Data Analyst posting:**
  - The role is to build a data-driven decision culture *within the silo* through short-cycle experiments and learning.
  - It requires A/B-test design and validation experience.
  - Source: [inthiswork](https://inthiswork.com/archives/344917)
- **Toss Place / analyst postings:**
  - The role is to "quantify team/silo goals as metrics, then set hypotheses, key levers and priorities" for fast iteration.
  - Postings mention CLV, retention and cohort analysis.

### Inferences
- **Common mechanism across Toss and 당근:** data culture runs through a **goal → metric → hypothesis → small experiment → shared result** chain owned by a small autonomous unit. It does not run through data collection as a standalone duty. Toss postings say this explicitly ("quantify team goals as metrics → hypotheses → key levers"). The user's team currently seems to get the *collection* step without the upstream goal → metric link or the downstream decision.
- **Shared definitions come before analysis.** Toss invests in a trusted, consistent single source of data and in templated metric definitions. For a 3–8 person marketing team, the low-cost equivalent is a one-page **metric dictionary** (definition, source, owner, cadence, the decision it informs), agreed jointly. This also addresses "the analysis isn't at the level or direction I want", which is often a definitional mismatch.
- **Even experts skip the discipline** (the 당근 PM). Practices need to be *ritualized*, e.g. a short weekly metric review and experiment log, rather than left to individual motivation. The "experiments become a few people's job" warning suggests rotating ownership across channel owners (performance, SNS, CRM), so data work does not collapse onto the lead or one "data person".
- **Reduce friction before raising expectations.** 배민's "data fatigue" finding (Grade D) matches the user's "it's annoying" symptom. Automate collection: connectors, a shared sheet, GA4 or ad-platform exports, AI-assisted querying. Ask humans to spend time on interpretation and decisions, not on data plumbing.
- **Transferability caveat:** these are large, well-funded product and tech companies with dedicated analysts, event-logging infrastructure and A/B platforms. They also have employer-branding reasons to present an idealized culture. For a small marketing team, transfer the *structure* (goal → metric → hypothesis → review ritual, shared definitions, small autonomous ownership), not the tooling scale. Rigorous A/B testing may be impractical for brand and content work. "Test-and-learn" with pre-stated hypotheses and simple before/after or holdout comparisons is the realistic version.
- **Combined with Q1:** the Korean-specific twist is that the lead must *explicitly explain purpose and background* (the 지시 명확성 deficit) and make it safe to challenge metrics (silence research). Otherwise even well-designed rituals become another 보고 to perform for the boss.
